\section*{2. Validation against Prior Methods and Component Ablations}

\textbf{Response and positioning (AC, R2--3, R3--1).}
We agree that an algorithm-level comparison is important because our primary
contribution is the planner rather than the low-level controller.  We have
therefore added controlled comparisons to the two most closely related
planning principles.  ActNeRF~\citep{actnerf} estimates view utility from the
RGB rendering variance of a NeRF ensemble and, in its original system, jointly
plans camera motion and object manipulation.  PB-NBV~\citep{pbnbv} represents
occupied/frontier voxels by ellipsoids and selects a freely executable camera
view by their weighted image-plane projections.  In contrast, our fixed-camera
in-hand setting admits only three object-reorientation primitives.  Ray-GPIS
scores candidate views from the geometry actually acquired: GP
uncertainty is aggregated around object-centered hit/miss rays and multiplied
by point-cloud novelty.  Thus, it can distinguish a reliable surface from an
observed but sparse or misaligned region, which neither pose coverage nor a
projected frontier alone explicitly represents.

\textbf{Controlled algorithm-level comparison.}
Rather than reproducing the different manipulation systems and reconstruction
representations of the prior methods, we adapt only their core NBV criteria.
All methods receive the same RGB-D observations, masks and object poses, 256
candidate directions, $\{-x,-y,+z\}$ primitives, NBV-to-action mapper, fusion
backend, and evaluator; only the planner changes.  The benchmark contains
eight objects, including concave and thin/irregular geometry, and 15 shared
initial orientations, giving 120 paired episodes.  Each episode contains
exactly one initial image followed by five planner-selected images.  ActNeRF's
three initialization seeds are first averaged within each paired scene.

We report F-score AUC over all six reconstruction states and final F-score at
5\,mm.  To evaluate the decision policy directly, we clone every state, execute
all three actions, and measure their realized one-step F-score gains.  We then
compute the Spearman correlation between planner scores and realized gains and
the regret relative to the best available action.  Planning time includes
representation updating and candidate scoring; for adapted ActNeRF it also
includes five-model ensemble updating and rendering.  Table~\ref{tab:main}
reports means and 95\% confidence intervals over the 120 paired scenes.  Timing
uses synchronized CUDA execution for the GPyTorch GP and ActNeRF ensemble;
PB-NBV is CPU-native and is measured on the same RTX~4090~D workstation.

\textbf{Results.}
Ray-GPIS improves F-score AUC over adapted ActNeRF by 0.03227 (95\% paired
bootstrap CI: 0.02258--0.04038), with 106/1/13 wins/ties/losses and a
Holm-corrected two-sided Wilcoxon $p=8.73\!\times\!10^{-15}$.  More
importantly, it raises score--gain correlation from 0.162 to 0.472 and reduces
oracle regret from 0.0317 to 0.0088, showing that the improvement comes from
selecting more informative actions rather than from a different reconstruction
backend.  It also plans in 0.261\,s, versus 2.238\,s for adapted ActNeRF
($8.6\times$ faster).  Final Recall@5 and Chamfer, omitted from the compact
table, show the same ordering; relative to ActNeRF, Ray-GPIS improves final
recall by 0.0326 and reduces Chamfer by 0.331\,mm.

PB-NBV falling below the fixed schedule is consistent with the action-space
adaptation rather than evidence that PB-NBV is generally inferior.  The fixed
schedule is action-native and deliberately contains two high-yield $-x$
rotations.  PB-NBV instead assumes that its selected free-view camera pose can
be executed directly; after quantization to only three in-hand primitives, its
frontier-projection and global-partition scores transfer poorly to realized
geometric gain, as indicated by the negative correlation and larger regret.
This is not an execution advantage for Ray-GPIS: every active planner uses the
same candidate set and mapper.  Under that identical constraint, Ray-GPIS
retains positive correlation and low regret.  We additionally swept PB-NBV's
normalized voxel sizes ($d/30$, $d/50$, and $d/70$) and its partition option,
and report the strongest tested configuration ($d/70$ with partitioning).

\textbf{Component ablations (AC, R1--1, R3--2).}
All ablations use the same paired clean benchmark and remove exactly one design
choice.  Novelty alone detects obvious gaps but cannot identify an observed yet
poorly constrained surface.  Uncertainty alone tends to revisit noisy known
regions, showing why novelty is needed to suppress redundant acquisition.  A
pointwise GP produces less stable, spatially fragmented scores and weaker
score--gain agreement than receptive-field ray aggregation.  Finally, using
hit rays only prevents the planner from assigning utility to directions with
no current surface return, delaying discovery of entirely unseen, bottom, or
concave regions.  The consistent reductions in F-score AUC and decision
quality support the complementary roles of uncertainty, novelty, ray
aggregation, and miss-ray interpolation in the full method.
