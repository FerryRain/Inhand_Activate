\section*{1. Contribution, Motivation, and Distinction from Generic NBV}

AURORA is motivated by how humans inspect partially observed objects: they re-orient an object in hand to expose hidden surfaces and adapt subsequent motions based on what remains unseen \textbf{(AC, R\_dWbG--2)}. We transfer this closed-loop strategy to a fixed-camera setting in which the grasped object is persistently occluded by the fingers. Unlike generic NBV methods that move a camera around a static object, AURORA uses contact-constrained in-hand rotation to change the object pose relative to the hand and reveal occluded regions. Ray-GPIS determines \emph{which} orientation should be explored, whereas Hora-like controllers address \emph{how} to execute the requested rotation; the two are therefore complementary \textbf{(R\_BKKY--2)}. Finally, we validate the practical value of the recovered geometry through downstream grasping, where active reconstruction improves success from \textbf{[YY]} and \textbf{[ZZ]} to \textbf{[XX]} compared with non-active and single-view reconstructions \textbf{(AC, R\_dWbG--1)}.

A simpler alternative is to maximize pose diversity \textbf{(AC, R\_BKKY--2)}. We therefore add a Pose-Novelty baseline that uses only tracked pose and maximizes the minimum spherical distance to previously visited viewing directions. This directly tests whether reconstruction conditioning is necessary beyond viewpoint coverage; Sec.~2 reports the resulting reconstruction AUC, realized geometric gain, and planner--score correlation.

\section*{2. Validation against Prior Methods and Component Ablations}


We agree that an algorithm-level comparison is important because our primary contribution lies in the planner rather than the low-level controller \textbf{(AC, R2--3, R3--1)}. Accordingly, we compare Fixed, Pose-Novelty, adapted ActNeRF~\citep{actnerf}, PB-NBV~\citep{pbnbv}, and Ray-GPIS using the same observations, object poses, 256 candidate views, three action primitives, fusion backend, and evaluator; only the planner changes. Pose-Novelty predicts each primitive's next object-frame viewing direction and maximizes its minimum angular distance to the pose history. We evaluate all methods over the same 120 paired episodes (eight objects, 15 poses, one initial image plus five selected images).

\textbf{Results.}
Compared with adapted ActNeRF, Ray-GPIS reduces planning time by 88.3\% while raising score--gain correlation from 0.162 to 0.472 and reducing regret from 0.0317 to 0.0088. PB-NBV transfers poorly from freely executable camera views to three quantized in-hand primitives, yielding negative score--gain correlation. \textbf{Why ideal pose coverage can fail in practice.}
Although Pose-Novelty leads in Table~\ref{tab:main}(a), ideal sensing makes a
recorded viewing direction nearly equivalent to acquired geometry. In
practice, pose error, RGB--depth misregistration, or hand occlusion can advance
pose history while local depth fails to fuse. Pose-only planning then marks the
direction as covered, whereas Ray-GPIS still sees a reconstruction gap. As
shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}(b), reconstruction-aware planning is more robust
to this visited-but-unacquired case. We therefore make this conditional claim,
rather than claiming universal dominance over pose coverage.

\textbf{Component robustness.}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}(b), miss-ray interpolation retains candidate
directions whose surfaces are entirely absent under palm occlusion, while
receptive-field integration prevents pose perturbations from fragmenting the
uncertainty score map. The one-component removals therefore show that Full
combines complementary robustness mechanisms.





\section*{3. Additional Evaluation and Clarifications}

\paragraph{Object Diversity (R\_4zfY--3)}
We select geometrically diverse objects within the feasible manipulation range of the current in-hand controller.

\paragraph{Pose and Execution Robustness (R\_BKKY--4)}
Table~\ref{tab:main}(b) shows that receptive-field integration stabilizes the score map under pose perturbations; the reconstruction--pose mismatch test further covers the distinct case in which tracking succeeds but local geometry acquisition fails.

\paragraph{Runtime and Replanning Frequency (R\_4zfY--Q)}
The online runtime breakdown is segmentation \textbf{[XX]}\,ms,
tracking/fusion \textbf{[XX]}\,ms, GP update \textbf{[XX]}\,ms, and
NBV/action selection \textbf{[XX]}\,ms. Thus, the 6\,s interval is
determined by manipulation and observation acquisition rather than
computation.

\paragraph{6s}The 6,s interval is the execution horizon of one tactile in-hand rotation primitive, not the latency of perception or planning. RGB-D tracking, keyframe selection, and fusion continue throughout the primitive, while the rotation axis is updated only at primitive boundaries; switching more frequently would provide insufficient viewpoint change and can destabilize the contact-maintaining controller. The planner itself requires only 0.261,s, so replanning is manipulation-limited rather than computation-limited.
